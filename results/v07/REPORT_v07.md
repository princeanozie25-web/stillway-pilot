# Stillway v0.7 report: learn a diagnostic from verified experience

For Prince Anozie, Astra and Codex. From Fable, 22 November 2026. Branch `v0.7`. Everything below is from development data. The final set is Codex's and has not been opened; the protocol and all artefacts are locked for it (`results/v07/LOCK_v07.json`, SHA-256 `deed5cff683e67fb…fa71aef`, made at commit `859a8a7`; `python v07/check_lock.py --model` reports ALL MATCH).

## 1. Where it stands

**The question.** Does experience from previous bugs increase the share of unfamiliar repositories a small local student fixes on the first submitted patch, under the same inference budget?

**The answer on development data: no.** Experience made the student far better at bugs it had seen and at the same cause in a new setting, and worse at everything else. Averaged over unfamiliar repositories the two cancel.

| First patch correct | Unchanged student | Trained on final patches | Trained on experience | Experience + bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact repeats (120 training repositories, 1 seed) | 12.5% | 100% | 93.3% | 86.7% |
| Renamed repeats (24) | 8.3% | 100% | 95.8% | 90.3% |
| Same cause, changed preconditions (16) | 25.0% | 50.0% | 62.5% | 72.9% |
| Lookalikes: trained symptom, different cause (16) | 25.0% | 8.3% | 6.2% | 6.2% |
| Mechanisms absent from training (16) | 25.0% | 2.1% | 6.2% | 14.6% |
| **All unfamiliar (48)** | **25.0%** | **20.1%** | **25.0%** | **31.2%** |

- The frozen primary rule asks for +10 points on unfamiliar repositories with the interval above zero. Measured: **+6.2 points, interval -10.4 to +22.9. It fails.**
- The recognition rule passes: +81.9 points on renamed repeats.
- By the rules frozen in `docs/v07_protocol.md`, the development verdict is a **recognition result**. In the brief's terms that earns a recognition result, not the larger-model comparison. The final set decides; I expect the same there and have said so in the protocol.

**Two findings matter more than the verdict.**

1. **The trained student learned fixes, not diagnosis.** On lookalikes it breaks a hidden test that passed before in 60% to 77% of repositories. The unchanged student breaks none.
2. **The harm is a matter of dose, and a small dose helps** (found after the plan was fixed; section 7). The same experience adapter at a quarter of its strength fixes 36% of unfamiliar repositories against 25%, is better than the unchanged student on 14 development repositories and worse on none, and adds almost no regressions. It keeps little of the gain on repeats. This was found on development data, so it counts for nothing until the final set, where it is now a separate rule.

| D-family experience adapter, no insert | Strength 0 (unchanged) | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 (as trained) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unfamiliar repositories fixed | 25.0% | 36.1% | 38.2% | 31.2% | 27.1% |
| Renamed repeats fixed | 8.3% | 25.0% | 47.2% | 81.9% | 91.7% |
| Unfamiliar repositories with a test broken | 2.1% | 5.6% | 16.7% | 31.9% | 35.4% |

## 2. What was built

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| Student | `Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct`, revision `2e1fd397`, weights SHA-256 `c1b9b30e…bc13a8`, Apache-2.0. 1.54 billion weights, 32-bit floats, no quantisation, on the laptop's GPU (M1 Max, 32 GB). 31 tokens a second. Pin: `results/v07/MODEL_PIN.json`. |
| Smoke test | Inference, four adapter updates (loss 1.46 to 0.007), save, reload with identical output, base output restored with the adapter off. `results/v07/SMOKE_MODEL.json`. |
| Training method | A low-rank adapter written by hand: rank 16 on all seven projection types in all 28 layers, 18.5 million weights (1.2% of the model). AdamW, 300 updates of at most 2,048 tokens, three seeds. About 17 minutes per adapter. |
| Bugs | Generated by me, ordinary functional bugs in small Python packages, standard library only. 12 training designs in 10 naming schemes (120 repositories); 12 development designs in 4 naming schemes never used in training (72 repositories). All 192 checked by execution: the bug fails a visible and a hidden test, the fix passes all, the old fix fails where it should. |
| Mechanisms | Three symptoms: a result empty on second use (iterator exhaustion), a derived value wrong after an update (stale derived values), an element missing at the edge (boundary errors). Each has two designs for the prior procedure, one for a local exception, one for a different cause behind the same symptom. |
| Episode | Two test scripts, each with a prediction written before it runs, then one patch that replaces one function. Same limits and tools for every arm. Tests run in a sandbox: no network, no writes outside a temporary folder, 5 seconds. |
| Teacher records | For each training repository: files, code hash, each proposed test, the prediction made before execution, the observed output, the patch, changed lines, timing, the verified outcome. 90 alternate executions from the same savepoint that failed or were contradicted are kept (the prior fix applied where the exception holds; a naive test whose prediction was wrong). `results/v07/records/teacher_traces.jsonl`. |
| Bank | Three diagnostics. Version 1 of each is the prior procedure with an explicit scope. Version 2 adds the local exception and what to check when a test contradicts the diagnostic. Both versions are kept, with the repositories behind each clause. When a prediction is contradicted during an episode the bank marks the diagnostic REOPENED and tells the student to verify the exception. 13 KB. `artifacts/v07/bank.json`. |

What the table says:

- **Experience against final patches.** Experience is ahead by about 5 points on unfamiliar repositories (interval includes zero) and behind by 4 on renamed repeats. With the bank it is ahead of final patches by 11 points, interval above zero.
- **The bank.** It adds about 4 points over the same student without it. All of that is in one group: same cause, changed preconditions (+12.5, interval +4.2 to +20.8, better on 6, worse on none). There the right diagnostic also beats a wrong one by 10 points. On lookalikes and absent mechanisms the bank, a wrong diagnostic, a retrieved example and nothing at all are indistinguishable.
- **One seed carries the bank's effect.** D minus F in repositories, by seed: -1, +1, +6.

### 4.4 After a contradiction

A contradiction is an episode in which at least one of the student's two tests did not come out as it predicted.

| Arm | Episodes with a contradiction | First patch correct after one | First patch correct with none |
|---|---|---|---|
| A unchanged | 65 of 72 | 18.5% | 28.6% |
| C experience | 151 of 216 | 35.1% | 80.0% |
| D experience + bank | 162 of 216 | 39.5% | 85.2% |
| F bank disabled | 158 of 216 | 36.1% | 82.8% |

- A contradicted prediction is a strong sign that the patch will be wrong: correctness roughly halves.
- **The reopening does not change what the student does.** On lookalikes the bank arm's prediction was contradicted in 47 of 48 episodes and the diagnostic was reopened each time. The student then submitted the trained fix anyway in 28 of them. Without the bank: 18 of 46.
- The exception clause does not help on its own ground either: on exact repeats the bank arm fails 6 of 10 repositories of the stale-value exception design, which the same student without the bank fixes 10 of 10.

### 4.5 Repeated use

The bank arm's correctness does not rise or fall with use within a run: 55.6% on the first 5 uses of a diagnostic, 50.0% on uses 6 to 15, 49.4% after that. The bank is not updated during evaluation, so this is a check for drift, and there is none.

### 4.6 Tests the student asked for, apart from hidden grading

| Arm | Its tests are runnable | Its predictions matched execution | Visible tests pass but hidden grading fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| A unchanged | 98.6% | 11.8% | 0.0% |

Experience training is what teaches the student to predict what a test will print (12% to 45%). Training on final patches does not (7%). Each episode makes exactly two tool calls in every arm. Hidden tests are run only by the grader, after the patch is submitted.

## 6. Decisions the plan fixed in advance

- **Sleep promotion was not tested.** The condition, written before any trained arm was evaluated, was that the bank adds at least 5 points on unfamiliar repositories. It adds 4.2, from one seed. There is no transferred procedure to promote, so the comparison with equal-cost rehearsal was not run.
- **All outcomes and seeds are kept.** 24 development runs of 72 episodes, 5 exact-repeat runs of 120, and 18 reduced-strength runs of 72. Nothing was dropped or rerun.

## 7. Beyond the brief

Three things tried after the development results. None is part of the frozen rules.

1. **The unchanged student given the bank, or the nearest example.** With the diagnostic it is unchanged (19.4% against 19.4%). With the nearest example it is worse (8.3%): only 17% of its patches apply, because it rewrites the example's function. So the bank's text does nothing for a student that was not trained to read it, and ordinary retrieval harms it.
2. **A familiarity gate** (`v07/gate.py`, `results/v07/dev_gate.md`). Send a repository to the trained student only if it resembles a training repository, otherwise to the unchanged one. The threshold was set from training repositories alone. 71 of 72 development repositories pass the gate, including 15 of 16 with mechanisms absent from training. Surface similarity cannot tell an unfamiliar bug from a familiar one. My first threshold rule was wrong (it let shared names outweigh design) and I corrected it before scoring anything.
3. **The adapters at reduced strength** (`v07/x_strength.py`, `results/v07/x_strength.md`). The adapter's contribution is multiplied by a number between 0 and 1 in every layer. No retraining, same episode, same budget. I tried 0.5 once on the D-family adapter to see whether the losses on lookalikes and absent mechanisms were a matter of dose. They were, so I ran the other two adapters at 0.5 as controls and 0.25 and 0.75 for the curve.

   - **There is a dose at which experience helps on unfamiliar repositories.** Success there rises to a peak at a quarter to a half of the trained strength and falls back; success on repeats and the rate of regressions both rise steadily with strength.
   - **At a quarter strength nothing is lost.** No development repository is fixed less often than by the unchanged student. The gains are real fixes where the unchanged student's patch applied and was wrong: comparing floats for equality (7 of 12 runs against 0), a mutable default argument, a key not normalised.
   - **Is it experience, or any light training?** Not settled. At half strength the two experience adapters are ahead of the final-patch adapter on unfamiliar repositories by 2 and 8 points, with intervals that include zero. On lookalikes the gap is larger (38% and 46% against 17%). The final-patch adapter at half strength keeps 99% on repeats, so it is the best single student over all 72 repositories.
   - **The bank hurts at half strength** (31.9% against 38.2% without it): on lookalikes it pulls the student back to the trained fix (46% of patches against 8%).
   - **How much to trust it.** 0.5 was the first value tried. 0.25 was picked from the curve. At half strength one seed carries much of the gain (16, 16 and 23 of 48 unfamiliar repositories, against 12 unchanged). At quarter strength the three seeds agree (19, 16 and 17). All six reduced-strength arms now run on the final set, with rules X1 to X5 written before it is opened; they are reported beside the verdict and cannot change it.

## 8. What I take from this

1. **A small student trained on 120 verified episodes learns the twelve fixes.** It recognises them under new names (90% to 100%) and carries them to the same cause in a new setting (60% to 73% against 25%).
2. **It pays for that with what it could already do.** On bugs outside its experience it falls from 25% to between 2% and 15%, and on lookalikes it does damage.
3. **Executed experience is better training material than final patches** in two measurable ways: predictions that match execution (45% against 7%) and transfer to changed preconditions (+12.5 points). Neither is enough to pass.
4. **The bank is read only where it agrees with what the student already learned.** Right diagnostic, wrong diagnostic and none differ only on changed preconditions. A reopened diagnostic does not stop the trained fix.
5. **A contradicted prediction is a real signal** (correctness halves) that nothing in the system acts on.

6. **How much of the experience is applied matters more than how it is stored.** The bank, the retrieved example and the wrong diagnostic moved unfamiliar success by 0 to 4 points. Strength moved it by 11 to 13.

If there is a v0.8, two tests come first. One: let the student choose its own dose per repository, using the signal that already exists (a contradicted prediction), full strength when its tests agree with it and low strength when they do not. Two: whether the damage is a property of the training set: add designs where the trained symptom has another cause and the correct move is to drop the diagnostic, and train the student to act on a contradiction. The lookalike and absent-mechanism groups already measure it.

## 9. What is not matched, and limits

- **Teacher cost** (outside every arm): I wrote all 24 designs, tests, fixes and the diagnostics' wording (about 2,100 lines). I am a far larger model than the student. B uses the fixes; C to W also use the teacher's tests and contradicted attempts; D and W also use the bank's wording.
- **Execution cost** (outside every arm): 196 seconds to build and verify all 192 repositories; 300 teacher test runs; 30 prior-fix executions that failed.
- **Training tokens.** Updates and budget match (300 of 2,048 tokens). Tokens used do not: about 383,000 for the D-family adapter against about 475,000 for B and C, because its examples are longer.
- **Prompt length.** Arms with an insert read up to 400 more prompt tokens.
- **One author.** Every development repository is mine. The lookalike and absent-mechanism groups are small (16 repositories, 4 designs each), and A's 25% in each is 4 repositories of one design. Family-level intervals are wide for that reason.
- **One student, one adapter size, one training length.** Reduced strength is a stand-in for gentler training, not the same thing; fewer updates or a lower learning rate were not tried.
- **Prompt.** Changed twice on 12 training repositories before any development repository was run.
- A has one run; decoding is greedy and deterministic.
